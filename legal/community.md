# Community Guidelines

FAULT has one public space: the registry of juror names on the leaderboards. Everything you type there is seen by every other player. These guidelines keep it a place people want to be.

## Juror names

Your juror name need not be your own — and we suggest it isn't. It must not:

- **impersonate** anyone: a real person, a public figure, a judge, an official, a FAULT moderator or staff member;
- contain **hate** — slurs, or attacks on people for their race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, caste, disability, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, or age;
- be **sexual**, obscene, or refer to sexual violence;
- **harass**, threaten or target a specific real person;
- promote **violence**, terrorism, self-harm, or crimes against children;
- include **personal information** — yours or anyone else's — such as a phone number, address, email or social handle;
- **advertise** or link to anything;
- try to get around the name filter with misspellings, symbols or look-alike characters.

The registry filters names automatically, but no filter is perfect. You can change your own name once a day in Settings.

## Reports

If you see a juror name that breaks these guidelines, press and hold it on the leaderboard and choose **Report**. You can also hide it from your own view straight away.

If a case seems to name a **real person**, is genuinely **harmful**, or doesn't make sense, use the **Report** button on the case. The case is withdrawn from play at once while we look at it.

Please report honestly. Filing false or abusive reports is itself a breach of these guidelines.

## What happens

Depending on how serious a breach is, we may rename or hide a name, remove content, or suspend or close an account. Serious or repeated breaches lead to permanent removal. Where the law requires, we may also report content to the authorities.

## The cases themselves

Every person in a FAULT case is fictional, and the game's cases are generated rather than written by players. Real places and courts appear as setting only. If you ever think a case crosses a line, reporting it is the most useful thing you can do.
